\section{Between-Subjects Survey Experiment}
\label{sec:method-survey}
We conducted a between-subjects survey experiment to examine how readers respond to discussions that include a minority judgment (\textbf{RQ4}). We compared exposure to two majority-aligned comment threads with exposure to one majority-aligned and one minority-aligned thread, measuring readers' judgments, perceived comment influence, and consideration of alternative perspectives.

\subsection{Survey Design}
\label{subsec:survey-design}

\paragraph{Design and stimuli}
Figure~\ref{fig:survey-procedure} (Appendix~\ref{app:survey}) illustrates the design and procedure. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four high-agreement AITA posts (two with a majority \textit{YA} judgment and two with a majority \textit{NA} judgment) and one of two comment-display conditions. The posts concerned financial obligations between friends, parenting, punctuality, and workplace conduct.

In the \textit{Majority--Majority} condition (MM), participants saw the two highest-scoring majority comments, reflecting the comments typically encountered first on high-agreement Reddit posts. In the \textit{Majority--Minority} condition (Mm), the highest-scoring minority comment replaced the second majority comment. Within each scenario, the post and Comment~1 were held constant; only Comment~2 and its reply thread varied. The manipulation therefore combined thread content, replies, and observed scores.

The Reddit-style interface retained the original wording, scores, and \textit{YA}/\textit{NA} judgments. Replies were nested up to four levels, initially collapsed, and expandable via \textit{View replies}. Usernames were replaced with generic labels; majority/minority labels and agreement percentages were omitted.

The sample comprises 80 participants (40 per condition). Participants read their assigned post and threads, reported a moral judgment with an explanation of at least two sentences, and completed the self-report measures below.

\paragraph{Measures}
Moral judgment, the primary outcome, used an eight-point scale from 1 (Definitely YA) to 8 (Definitely NA); judgment certainty used a four-point scale (1--4). Per-comment influence, agreement, and perspective consideration used five-point scales (1--5). Participants also identified which comment contributed more to their judgment (Comment~1, Comment~2, both equally, or neither). Three seven-point items assessed discussion-level perspective broadening: considering both sides, encountering new considerations, and engaging with reasons supporting an alternative judgment. Display preference contrasted two highly upvoted comments supporting the same judgment with two representing opposing judgments, plus a neither option.

\paragraph{Perspective broadening and consideration}
Participants in the Mm condition reported higher overall perspective broadening than those in the MM condition ($M=4.89$ versus $M=4.08$ on the seven-point measure; $\Delta=0.81$, $p=.012$). Comment-level perspective consideration was also descriptively higher in Mm for both Comment~1 ($M=2.50$ versus $M=1.98$; $p=.051$) and Comment~2 ($M=2.48$ versus $M=2.00$; $p=.072$). These comment-level comparisons did not meet the conventional $.05$ threshold. The results thus suggest greater self-reported broadening at the discussion level, while the evidence for differences attributed to individual comments is less conclusive.

\paragraph{Perceived influence and agreement}
Agreement with Comment~2 was lower in Mm than in MM ($M=2.78$ versus $M=4.03$; $\Delta=-1.25$, $p<.001$), whereas agreement with the shared majority Comment~1 was similar descriptively ($M=3.90$ versus $M=3.95$; $p=.852$). Perceived influence was descriptively higher for Comment~1 in Mm ($M=2.83$ versus $M=2.28$; $p=.056$) and lower for Comment~2 ($M=1.95$ versus $M=2.20$; $p=.313$). Neither influence comparison met the $.05$ threshold. Higher overall perspective broadening therefore did not coincide with higher mean agreement with, or perceived influence of, the minority comment.

\paragraph{Judgment certainty}
The reported judgment-certainty measure was lower in Mm than in MM ($M=2.68$ versus $M=3.15$; reported $\Delta=-0.48$, $p=.037$).

Taken together, the current summaries suggest that discussions including a minority thread can broaden reported consideration of alternative perspectives without securing greater agreement with that thread. Because the manipulation replaced an entire thread, the comparison does not identify which arguments, replies, or score cues account for the observed differences. Interpretation also remains preliminary pending fuller reporting of uncertainty, multiple comparisons, and variation across scenarios.
