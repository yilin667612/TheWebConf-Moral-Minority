\section{Between-Subjects Survey Experiment}
\label{sec:method-survey}
We conducted a between-subjects survey experiment to examine how readers respond to discussions that include a minority judgment (\textbf{RQ4}). We compared exposure to two majority-aligned comment threads with exposure to one majority-aligned and one minority-aligned thread, measuring readers' judgments, perceived comment influence, and consideration of alternative perspectives.

\subsection{Survey Design}
\label{subsec:survey-design}

\paragraph{Design, scenarios, and stimuli}
Figure~\ref{fig:survey-procedure} (Appendix~\ref{app:survey}) illustrates the experimental design and procedure. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four moral-dilemma scenarios (two with a majority \textit{YA} judgment and two with a majority \textit{NA} judgment) and one of two comment-display conditions. All four scenarios were drawn from high-agreement AITA posts and covered financial responsibility between friends, family and parenting responsibilities, punctuality and obligations in friendships, and professional conduct in the workplace.

For each post, we selected the two highest-scoring majority comments and the highest-scoring minority comment. In the \textit{Majority--Majority} condition (MM), participants saw the two majority comments. This condition was designed to reflect the two comments readers typically encounter first on high-agreement Reddit posts. In the \textit{Majority--Minority} condition (Mm), participants instead saw the highest-scoring majority comment and the minority comment. Comment~1 was the same majority comment in both conditions, while Comment~2 was either the second majority comment or the minority comment. The comparison thus held the post and Comment~1 constant while replacing the thread rooted at Comment~2. It assessed the combined presentation of the selected thread's arguments, replies, and observed score, rather than the effect of minority alignment independently of these features.

Posts and comments were presented in a Reddit-style interface using their original wording, with observed scores and explicit \textit{YA} or \textit{NA} judgments displayed. Replies were preserved in their original nested structure up to four levels deep, collapsed by default, and expandable via \textit{View replies}. Usernames were replaced with generic labels. Comments were not explicitly labelled as majority or minority, and numerical majority percentages were not shown. Thus, the manipulation captured exposure to naturally occurring majority- or minority-aligned discussion threads rather than explicit alignment labels.

The current sample comprises 80 participants, with 40 in each comment-display condition.

The procedure consisted of three phases. First, participants read an AITA post and its discussion thread. Second, they reported their moral judgment and briefly explained their reasoning. Finally, they completed self-report measures of comment influence, agreement, reflection, perspective broadening, and comment-display preference.

\paragraph{Measures}

The primary outcome was participants' moral judgment of the original poster, measured on an eight-point scale from 1 (Definitely YA) to 8 (Definitely NA). Judgment certainty was measured on a four-point scale (1--4). Participants also provided an open-ended explanation of at least two sentences. For each featured comment, participants rated perceived influence, agreement, and perspective consideration, each on a five-point scale (1--5). They also indicated whether Comment~1, Comment~2, both equally, or neither contributed more to their judgment. Discussion-level perspective broadening was measured using three seven-point items assessing whether the discussion encouraged consideration of both sides, introduced new considerations, and prompted engagement with reasons supporting an alternative judgment. Finally, participants indicated their preferred comment-display format: two highly upvoted comments supporting the same judgment, two highly upvoted comments representing opposing judgments, or neither.

\paragraph{Perspective broadening and consideration}
Participants in the Mm condition reported higher overall perspective broadening than those in the MM condition ($M=4.89$ versus $M=4.08$ on the seven-point measure; $\Delta=0.81$, $p=.012$). Comment-level perspective consideration was also descriptively higher in Mm for both Comment~1 ($M=2.50$ versus $M=1.98$; $p=.051$) and Comment~2 ($M=2.48$ versus $M=2.00$; $p=.072$). These comment-level comparisons did not meet the conventional $.05$ threshold. The results thus suggest greater self-reported broadening at the discussion level, while the evidence for differences attributed to individual comments is less conclusive.

\paragraph{Perceived influence and agreement}
Agreement with Comment~2 was lower in Mm than in MM ($M=2.78$ versus $M=4.03$; $\Delta=-1.25$, $p<.001$), whereas agreement with the shared majority Comment~1 was similar descriptively ($M=3.90$ versus $M=3.95$; $p=.852$). Perceived influence was descriptively higher for Comment~1 in Mm ($M=2.83$ versus $M=2.28$; $p=.056$) and lower for Comment~2 ($M=1.95$ versus $M=2.20$; $p=.313$). Neither influence comparison met the $.05$ threshold. Higher overall perspective broadening therefore did not coincide with higher mean agreement with, or perceived influence of, the minority comment.

\paragraph{Judgment certainty}
The reported judgment-certainty measure was lower in Mm than in MM ($M=2.68$ versus $M=3.15$; reported $\Delta=-0.48$, $p=.037$).

Taken together, the current summaries suggest that discussions including a minority thread can broaden reported consideration of alternative perspectives without securing greater agreement with that thread. Because the manipulation replaced an entire thread, the comparison does not identify which arguments, replies, or score cues account for the observed differences. Interpretation also remains preliminary pending fuller reporting of uncertainty, multiple comparisons, and variation across scenarios.
