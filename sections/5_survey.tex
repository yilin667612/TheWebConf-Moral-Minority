\section{Between-Subjects Survey Experiment}
\label{sec:method-survey}
We conducted a between-subjects survey experiment to examine how readers respond to discussions that include a minority judgment (\textbf{RQ4}). We compared exposure to two majority-aligned comment threads with exposure to one majority-aligned and one minority-aligned thread, measuring readers' judgments, perceived comment influence, and consideration of alternative perspectives.

\subsection{Survey Design}
\label{subsec:survey-design}

\runinheading{Design and stimuli}
Participants were randomly assigned to one of four high-agreement AITA posts (two with a majority \textit{YA} judgment and two with a majority \textit{NA} judgment) and one of two comment-display conditions. The posts concerned financial obligations between friends, parenting, punctuality, and workplace conduct. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:survey-posts} details the scenarios and allocation; Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:survey-procedure} shows the procedure.

In the \textit{Majority--Majority} condition (MM), participants saw the two highest-scoring majority comments. In the \textit{Majority--Minority} condition (Mm), the highest-scoring minority comment replaced the second majority comment. Within each scenario, the post and Comment~1 were held constant; only Comment~2 and its reply thread varied. The manipulation therefore combined thread content, replies, and observed scores.

The Reddit-style interface retained the original wording, scores, and \textit{YA}/\textit{NA} judgments. Replies were nested up to four levels, initially collapsed, and expandable via \textit{View replies}. Usernames were replaced with generic labels; majority/minority labels and agreement percentages were omitted.

\runinheading{Participants and procedure}
We recruited 80 participants through the online platform Prolific, with fluency in English as an eligibility criterion. Participants were aged 19–58 years (M = 34.10, SD = 9.27), with 40 identifying as male and 40 as female. Most participants were aged 25–34 (55.0\%). Participants completed the study in an average of 9 minutes and received €1 as compensation.

\runinheading{Measures}
Moral judgment, the primary outcome, used an eight-point scale from 1 (Definitely YA) to 8 (Definitely NA), with four certainty levels in each direction. Each selected response thus encoded both judgment direction and certainty. We recoded certainty from 1 (least certain) to 4 (most certain), assigning the two innermost responses to 1 and the two endpoints to 4. This reader-selected certainty differs from the linguistic certainty annotated in comments. Per-comment influence, agreement, and perspective consideration used five-point scales (1--5). Participants also identified which comment contributed more to their judgment (Comment~1, Comment~2, both equally, or neither). Three seven-point items assessed discussion-level perspective broadening: considering both sides, encountering new considerations, and engaging with reasons supporting an alternative judgment. Display preference contrasted two highly upvoted comments supporting the same judgment with two representing opposing judgments, plus a neither option.
Additional survey materials are described in Appendix~\ref{app:survey}.

\runinheading{Readers shown opposing judgments report broader perspective consideration.}
Readers in Mm reported higher mean discussion-level perspective broadening
and greater perspective consideration for each comment than readers in MM
(Figure~\ref{fig:survey-results}). This pattern appeared in both reported
judgment groups across the four scenarios.

\runinheading{Certainty differs with judgment alignment.}
Among readers whose reported judgments agreed with the majority, mean
certainty was lower in Mm than in MM (2.61 vs.\ 3.43). Among those agreeing
with the minority, mean certainty was higher in Mm (2.89 vs.\ 2.30).
Thus, broader perspective consideration accompanied lower certainty among
majority-aligned readers and higher certainty among minority-aligned readers.

Agreement with and perceived influence of Comment~2 were lower in Mm among
majority-aligned readers but higher among minority-aligned readers.
Agreement with the shared Comment~1 was similar across conditions, while
its perceived influence was higher in Mm in both groups. Participants in
both conditions most often preferred seeing one comment from each side
(MM: 67.5\%; Mm: 57.5\%).

For \textbf{RQ4}, the condition including a minority judgment is associated
with broader reported consideration of perspectives, including among
readers whose final judgment agrees with the majority. Certainty and
responses to the replacement comment vary with judgment alignment.
These exploratory comparisons describe responses to four selected posts
with their displayed threads and scores; judgment alignment was measured
after exposure. The figure reports 16 two-sided Welch's independent-samples
$t$-tests with unadjusted $p$-values.
