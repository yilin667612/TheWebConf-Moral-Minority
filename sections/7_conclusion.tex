\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We examined minority voices in online moral discussions through their
reception, the perspectives exchanged in replies, and readers' responses
to dissent. In AITA and AITAH, minority judgments received lower scores
but attracted more replies and deeper discussion under high agreement.
Blaming dissent drew more engagement than defending dissent. In the
annotated AITA discussions, challenges predominated in replies to both
minority directions. Replies to minority comments more often added
perspectives than those to majority comments; challenges did so more
frequently than support, while both introduced considerations absent
from their parent comments.

In the survey, readers shown a minority thread reported broader
perspective consideration in both final judgment groups than readers
shown two majority threads. Majority-aligned readers also reported lower
certainty, showing that broader consideration can accompany agreement
with the majority. Together, these findings distinguish social approval
from the perspectives developed around dissent and considered by readers.
They motivate designs that make competing judgments and their reply
context accessible alongside highly approved comments.
