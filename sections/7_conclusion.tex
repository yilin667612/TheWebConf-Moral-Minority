\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We examined minority voices in online moral discussions through their
reception, the moral considerations introduced in replies, and readers'
responses to dissent. In AITA, minority judgments received lower scores
but attracted more replies and deeper discussion than majority judgments,
with larger gaps under high agreement. Blaming dissent drew greater
engagement than defending dissent; these reception patterns also appeared
in AITAH. Annotated AITA replies predominantly challenged both minority
directions. Preliminary perspective annotations showed that replies to
minority comments more often added considerations absent from their
parents. Challenges did so more frequently than support, although both
orientations contributed additional reasons. The contribution of minority
voices can therefore include the exchanges around them, as well as the
reasoning they express themselves.

In the exploratory survey, readers shown one majority and one minority
thread reported a broader range of moral considerations than readers
shown two majority threads, including among those whose final judgments
agreed with the majority. Both comments also received higher mean ratings
for new considerations. These findings distinguish agreement with
a judgment from recognizing additional reasons for consideration. They
motivate evaluating comment displays by the reasons readers encounter
and use alongside social approval, and examining how access to minority
threads can support that process.
