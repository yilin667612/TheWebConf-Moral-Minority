\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We examined minority voices in online moral discussions through their
reception, the moral reasons introduced in replies, and readers'
responses to dissent. In AITA, minority judgments received lower scores
but attracted more replies and deeper discussion than majority judgments,
with larger gaps under high agreement. Blaming dissent drew greater
engagement than defending dissent; these reception patterns also appeared
in AITAH. Annotated AITA replies predominantly challenged both minority
directions. Across 100 high-agreement AITA posts, replies to defending
dissent had the highest observed overall addition rate. Challenges
introduced moral reasons absent from their parents more often than support
in every parent group. Minority participation therefore encompasses the
reasoning developed in response to dissent, as well as the reasons
dissenters express themselves.

In the exploratory survey, readers shown one majority and one minority
thread reported higher mean ratings for considering multiple sides, new
considerations, and reasons for alternative judgments than readers shown
two majority threads. Among readers whose final judgments aligned with the
majority, certainty was also lower in Balanced. These findings suggest that
considering alternative moral reasons can coexist with agreement with the
majority. They motivate evaluating comment displays by the reasons readers
encounter and use in their judgments, alongside social approval.
