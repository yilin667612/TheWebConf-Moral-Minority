\section{Data and Methods}
\label{sec:data-method}

\subsection{Data and Measures}
\label{subsec:dataset}

We analyze public AITA and AITAH archives containing submissions, comments,
verdict labels, scores, timestamps, and parent relationships. Documented
coverage extends from June 2013 for AITA and March 2021 for AITAH through
December 2025. The main reception cohort includes all eligible posts with
at least $200$ retained top-level verdicts, without post subsampling.
Source processing excludes empty or removed submission bodies;
AutoModerator has no verdict label and does not enter verdict counts.
These records represent retained archive activity, not a community census.
The reception frame contains $28{,}107$ AITA and $8{,}217$ AITAH posts
before exact-tie exclusion; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-dimensions}
provides further sample-accounting information.

A \emph{top-level comment} responds to the original post. Its immediate
children are \emph{direct replies}; all descendants form its reply subtree.
We use top-level verdicts to define the judgment distribution and nested
replies to measure responses to those judgments. Structural measures use
stored labels and linked replies without requiring usable reply text or
verifiable authors; annotation applies additional eligibility criteria.

\paragraph{Verdict alignment.}
We assign the first recognized verdict in textual order, matching short
codes case-sensitively at word boundaries and full phrases case-insensitively.
After normalizing hypothetical forms and H-suffixed aliases, we group
$\mathrm{YA}=\{\mathrm{YTA},\mathrm{ESH}\}$ and
$\mathrm{NA}=\{\mathrm{NTA},\mathrm{NAH}\}$, excluding INFO and
unrecognized comments. ESH (``Everyone Sucks Here'') assigns blame to both
parties; NAH (``No Assholes Here'') assigns it to neither; INFO requests
more information. Hypothetical YWBTA/YWNBTA map to YTA/NTA, and
H-suffixed variants map to their base tags. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-tags}
lists all forms. For post $p$, define
\[
N(p)=N_{\mathrm{YA}}(p)+N_{\mathrm{NA}}(p),\qquad
\rho(p)=N_{\mathrm{YA}}(p)/N(p).
\]
The dominant verdict $D(p)$ is YA when $\rho(p)>.5$ and NA when
$\rho(p)<.5$; exact ties are excluded from alignment-based analyses.
Majority comments match $D(p)$; minority comments oppose it.
High agreement denotes $\rho(p)\le.2$ or $\rho(p)\ge.8$; low agreement
denotes $.4\le\rho(p)\le.6$, excluding ties. Intermediate bands enter
only continuous and all-agreement analyses.

Blaming dissent is minority YA in NA-dominant posts; defending dissent is
minority NA in YA-dominant posts. Directional comparisons thus involve
different story cohorts. Alignment remains fixed to stored final observed
verdict metadata, including in temporal analyses; it does not measure the
opinion distribution perceived by commenters or an official community verdict.
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:dimensions} summarizes the dimensions and measures.

\paragraph{Reception.}
We measure comment score (last recorded upvotes minus downvotes),
direct-reply count (immediate children), and maximum reply depth (levels
below the verdict comment, with zero for no replies).
We first average
comments within each post and alignment group, then average these post-level
means with equal weight. Absent sides are undefined, not zero; missing
scores affect only score means. Group means may consequently involve
different contributing post sets and are not necessarily paired contrasts.
Scores and reply measures use available archive-wide
outcomes rather than a common follow-up duration. Additional reception
measures and arrival-hour analyses are described in
Appendices~\ref{sec:appendix-aita-agreement-gradient}
and~\ref{sec:appendix-aita-arrival}.

\subsection{Reply-Pair and Certainty Annotation}
\label{subsec:method-annotation}

\paragraph{Direct responses.}
The annotation unit is a top-level verdict comment paired with one immediate
reply. Given the original post as context, DeepSeek labels the reply as
support, challenge, information-seeking, or other relative to its parent,
not the OP or crowd. The prompt includes neither scores nor alignment
metadata. We exclude OP-authored parents or replies, same-author exchanges,
unverifiable identities, and pairs lacking usable post, parent, or reply text.

We draw three seeded samples of $16{,}000$ pairs each. Each sample contains
$2{,}000$ pairs from each of eight strata defined by community (AITA or
AITAH), the dominant verdict in high-agreement posts (NA or YA), and the
parent comment's alignment with that verdict (majority or minority).
Pairs come from a broader response frame not assumed to satisfy the
$200$-verdict threshold. Sampling is without replacement within strata,
but samples can overlap and have no post/parent caps. Response proportions
therefore weight pairs and condition on receiving an eligible reply,
unlike post-equal reception means. Challenge denotes disagreement, not
necessarily personal attack. Model annotations underwent development-set
review, not independent held-out validation. Support endorses the parent's
core position, challenge disputes it, information-seeking requests
clarification, and other covers remaining functions. Qualified agreement
can remain support; rhetorical rebuttals count as challenge. Annotation
documentation and validation status are summarized in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-annotation-details}.

\paragraph{Perspectives in replies.}
We separately annotate pairs of top-level comments and their direct replies
to identify whether the reply introduces a consideration absent from its
parent and, if so, the type of perspective introduced. The original post supplies context.
Novelty is relative to the parent, not necessarily to the entire discussion,
and is independent of support/challenge. Comparisons group parents by
majority/minority alignment and their blaming/defending stance toward the OP.

\paragraph{Expressed certainty.}
We sample $3\%$ of high-agreement and $15\%$ of low-agreement posts from
the $200$-verdict cohort and annotate their top-level comments individually.
The low/high sample contains $1{,}195$ posts and $665{,}107$ valid
YA/NA certainty records across the two communities.

The analysis retains the existing export's membership; additional draws
exclude previously annotated posts within each community and agreement band.
Certainty ranges from very uncertain ($1$), somewhat uncertain ($2$) and a somewhat certain ($3$), to a
very certain judgment ($4$). The same criteria apply across verdicts
and measure expressed language, not private confidence. Verdict alignment
continues to follow the first-match rule. Independent held-out human
validation and immutable model-version documentation remain unresolved;
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-annotation-details} summarizes the documentation
and validation status.

Full-period directional contrasts compare post-equal certainty distributions,
including the proportion at level $4$, without an arrival-time cutoff.
Their estimation and sensitivity analyses appear in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-certainty-comparison}. The differing sampling
fractions are not used to estimate community-wide certainty.

\paragraph{Certainty across discussion stages.}
We order each high-agreement
AITA post's retained first-$24$-hour verdict comments by creation time,
breaking ties by comment ID, and divide them into ten approximately
equal-count stages. Stages represent relative position in the comment
sequence rather than elapsed hours. We average certainty within each post and judgment group
(majority, blaming minority, or defending minority), then weight posts
equally. Each judgment group uses a separate fixed set of posts with valid certainty
observations for that group in every stage. A stage without such observations
is undefined, not zero. Pointwise $95\%$ intervals resample whole posts
($4{,}000$ replicates). These profiles describe successive comments, not
within-person change, and select discussions with persistent observed
expression. Exact stage assignment and additional all-agreement trend
analyses appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-certainty-progress}.
