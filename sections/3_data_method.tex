\section{Data and Methods}
\label{sec:data-method}

We analyze public archives from r/AmItheAsshole (AITA) and r/AITAH (AITAH),
including posts, comments, replies, judgment labels, scores, timestamps, and reply structure.
We identify perspectives in comments and replies and annotate expressed
certainty in comments. Figure~\ref{fig:study-overview}
illustrates the discussion structure, reception measures, and annotations.
Annotation sampling and procedures are detailed in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-annotation-details}.

\subsection{Data and Measures}
\label{subsec:dataset}

A \emph{post} is its original poster's (OP's) account of an interpersonal
conflict. A \emph{comment} is a top-level response to the post;
a \emph{reply} responds directly to a comment. A \emph{reply thread}
includes these replies and further nested responses.
The archives cover June 2013--December 2025 for AITA and
March 2021--December 2025 for AITAH, excluding posts with empty or removed bodies.

\paragraph{Judgments.}
A \emph{judgment} assesses the OP's conduct. We group YTA
(``You're the Asshole'') and ESH (``Everyone Sucks Here'') as \emph{YA},
assigning fault to the OP; NTA (``Not the Asshole'') and NAH
(``No Assholes Here'') form \emph{NA}, assigning no fault to the OP.
We use each comment's first recognized judgment
(matching rules and variants in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-tags}).
Judgment counts exclude INFO (requests for information), unrecognized
comments, and AutoModerator messages. Reception analyses retain posts
with at least 200 judgments: 28,107 in AITA and 8,217 in AITAH.

\paragraph{Majority, minority, and agreement.}
The \emph{dominant judgment} is a post's more frequent category, YA or NA.
\emph{Majority comments} match it; \emph{minority comments} oppose it.
Comparisons requiring this distinction exclude exact ties.
\emph{High agreement} means at least 80\% favor one side;
\emph{low agreement} means each side accounts for 40--60\%.
Intermediate posts enter only analyses spanning the agreement range.

\emph{Blaming dissent} assigns fault to an OP (\emph{YA}) whom the majority defends (\emph{NA});
\emph{defending dissent} defends an OP (\emph{NA}) whom the majority blames (\emph{YA}).
These directions concern different post sets. All classifications use
final observed judgments and remain fixed in temporal analyses, rather
than reflecting the distribution visible when a comment appeared or
the community's official judgment.

\paragraph{Reception measures.}
\emph{Comment score} is the last recorded net score (upvotes minus downvotes).
\emph{Reply count} counts direct replies; \emph{maximum reply depth}
is the deepest level beneath a comment (zero without replies,
one for replies without further nesting). These measures use stored judgment labels
and reply structure, without annotation's text and author eligibility filters.

We average comments within each post and judgment group, then weight
posts equally. Empty groups are omitted, not assigned zero; missing
scores affect only score averages. Contributing post sets may therefore
differ between groups. Outcomes use all available observations rather
than a uniform follow-up period. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:dimensions}
summarizes the measures; Appendices~\ref{sec:appendix-aita-agreement-gradient}
and~\ref{sec:appendix-aita-arrival} detail comparisons across agreement
and comment arrival time.

\subsection{Reply Annotation}
\label{subsec:method-annotation}

\paragraph{Support and challenge.}
We use the annotated samples of 16,000 comment--reply pairs from
high-agreement posts, drawn separately from the reception
sample and excluding OP participation. Given the post as context, DeepSeek
labels each reply's stance toward its comment, with human review of
100 development-set pairs. Labels distinguish \emph{support}, which endorses
the comment's position; \emph{challenge}, which disputes it;
\emph{information-seeking}, which requests clarification; and \emph{other},
which covers remaining functions. Challenge denotes disagreement, not necessarily
personal attack. Proportions weight eligible comment--reply pairs equally
and describe reply composition, not all comments.

\paragraph{Perspectives in replies.}
We analyze 7,023 eligible comment--reply pairs from 100 high-agreement
AITA posts, reusing their support/challenge labels. A \emph{perspective}
is an argumentative consideration represented by a category in a taxonomy
derived separately for each post. An LLM constructs this taxonomy and
labels each comment and reply independently; each text may receive
multiple categories or none. A reply adds a perspective when it receives
a category absent from its comment. This measures addition relative to
that comment, not novelty across the discussion, independently of
support/challenge. Within each post and majority/minority group,
proportions include all eligible replies, including those assigned no
category; groups without replies are omitted. We then average these
proportions across posts with equal weight.

\subsection{Expressed Certainty}
\label{subsec:method-certainty}

We use comment-level certainty annotations from sampled high- and
low-agreement posts. The scale captures
expressed language, not private confidence: very uncertain (1), somewhat
uncertain (2), somewhat certain (3), and very certain (4). The same
criteria apply across judgments.

We compare certainty distributions and the proportion at level 4 between
blaming and defending dissent over the full observed period, weighting
posts equally. These estimates describe the annotated sample, not
community-wide certainty.

\paragraph{Certainty across discussion stages.}
For high-agreement AITA posts, we divide the first 24 hours of judgment
comments into ten chronological stages containing approximately equal
numbers of comments. We compare majority, blaming minority, and defending
minority comments using equal post weights. For each group, we retain a
fixed set of posts with at least one comment from that group with a valid
certainty annotation in every stage. The curves therefore
describe successive comments in these selected discussions, not changes
within individuals. Estimation, sensitivity checks, and stage construction
are detailed in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-certainty}.
