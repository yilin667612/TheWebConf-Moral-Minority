\section{Data and Methods}
\label{sec:data-method}

\subsection{Data and Samples}
\label{subsec:dataset}

We analyze public AITA and AITAH archives linking submissions, comments,
verdict labels, scores, timestamps, and parent relationships. Documented
coverage runs from June 2013 for AITA and March 2021 for AITAH through
December 2025. Table~\ref{tab:analytical-samples} reports archive sizes,
analysis-specific samples, and annotation exclusions.

A \emph{top-level comment} responds to the original post; its immediate
children are \emph{direct replies}, and all descendants form its reply
subtree. Only top-level verdict comments define the judgment distribution;
nested replies measure responses to those judgments. The OP may participate
at either level.

The main reception cohort contains all eligible posts with at least $200$
retained verdicts, without post subsampling. Empty or removed submission
bodies are excluded during source processing. AutoModerator has no verdict
label and does not enter verdict counts. Structural measures use stored
labels and linked replies without requiring usable reply text or an author
filter; annotation imposes additional text and identity requirements.
These samples describe retained archive records, not a census of activity.

Certainty annotation samples $3\%$ of high-agreement and $15\%$ of
low-agreement posts from this cohort. Posts are sampled, but their top-level
comments are annotated individually. We retain the existing export's
membership and supplement earlier annotations without replacement within
community and agreement band. The all-agreement stage analysis additionally
uses intermediate-agreement posts from the broader annotation archive.
Neither analysis reweights observations to estimate community-wide certainty.
Direct-response annotation instead uses three seeded, stratified pair
samples from a broader high-agreement frame; these are not assumed to meet
the reception cohort's $200$-verdict threshold.

\subsection{Verdict Alignment}
\label{subsec:method}

A comment receives the first recognized verdict in textual order. Short
codes use case-sensitive word boundaries; full phrases are matched
case-insensitively. We normalize hypothetical forms and H-suffixed aliases,
then group canonical tags as $\mathrm{YA}=\{\mathrm{YTA},\mathrm{ESH}\}$
and $\mathrm{NA}=\{\mathrm{NTA},\mathrm{NAH}\}$, excluding INFO and
unrecognized comments (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-tags}).
For retained top-level counts, define
\[
N(p)=N_{\mathrm{YA}}(p)+N_{\mathrm{NA}}(p),\qquad
\rho(p)=N_{\mathrm{YA}}(p)/N(p).
\]
The dominant verdict $D(p)$ is YA for $\rho(p)>0.5$ and NA for
$\rho(p)<0.5$; exact ties are excluded. Majority comments match $D(p)$;
minority comments oppose it. High agreement means $\rho(p)\le.2$ or
$\rho(p)\ge.8$; low agreement means $.4\le\rho(p)\le.6$, excluding ties.
Intermediate bands enter the continuous and all-agreement analyses only.

Blaming dissent is minority YA in NA-dominant posts; defending dissent is
minority NA in YA-dominant posts. The two directions therefore involve
different story cohorts. Alignment uses stored final observed verdict
metadata throughout, including temporal analyses, not an official verdict
or the opinion distribution perceived by commenters.

\subsection{Reception and Responses}
\label{subsec:method-reception}

Score is the last recorded net vote count (upvotes minus downvotes), not
a score measured at a common comment age. Direct-reply count measures
immediate children; maximum reply depth counts levels below the verdict
comment, with zero for no replies. Reply trees use recorded parent links
and all observed replies, without an elapsed-time cutoff.

We average comments within each post and alignment group, then average
post-level means with equal post weight. Absent sides are undefined, not
zero; missing scores are excluded only from score means. Contributing post
sets may consequently differ, so majority--minority differences are not
paired contrasts. Directional comparisons use each post type's majority
baseline; Figure~\ref{fig:dissent-direction} combines both majority cohorts.
Recorded outcomes need not represent complete lifetime reception
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-Retrieval Timing}).

Arrival-hour comparisons group parent comments within $[0,18)$ hours into
hourly bins, retaining archive-wide scores and replies. Post-level means
are summarized separately for high-agreement majority and minority comments,
and jointly for both sides under low agreement. Empty cells are excluded.
Displayed percentile bands describe post-mean distributions, not confidence
intervals; display settings appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-aitah-arrival}.

\paragraph{Direct responses.}
\label{subsec:method-rq2}
Each annotation pairs a top-level verdict comment with one immediate reply.
With the original post as context, the reply is labeled support, challenge,
information-seeking, or other relative to its parent, not the OP or crowd.
The prompt supplies no scores or alignment metadata. We exclude OP-authored
parents or replies, same-author exchanges, and unverifiable identities.

Sampling is without replacement within each community--agreement-tail--
alignment stratum, but samples may overlap and have no post/parent caps.
Response proportions therefore weight pairs, unlike post-equal reception
means, and condition on receiving a reply. A challenge proportion is not
the probability that a comment receives a challenge, nor a measure of
personal attack. DeepSeek annotations underwent development-set review,
not independent held-out validation. Prompt, execution settings, and review
limits appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-rq2-annotation}.

\paragraph{OP participation.}
\label{subsec:method-op-participation}
Separately, we match comment and submission authors to measure any recorded
OP participation and first-comment timing, excluding unavailable identities
and AutoModerator. These summaries use the full eligible $200$-verdict
cohort, not the response-annotation sample
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-op-participation}).

\subsection{Expressed Certainty}
\label{subsec:method-certainty-contrasts}

Certainty labels range from unresolved/conflicting judgments ($1$), through
a leaning allowing alternatives ($2$) and a qualified judgment ($3$), to a
confidently asserted judgment ($4$). They measure expressed language, not
private confidence; the same criteria apply to both dissent directions.
Verdict alignment still follows the first-match rule. Annotation details
appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-annotation}.

The primary comparison uses all observed minority comments with valid
labels, without an arrival-time cutoff. We average within-post certainty
distributions equally across posts, excluding posts without valid minority
observations. Blaming-minus-defending contrasts emphasize the proportion
at level $4$, with mean certainty as a complementary summary. Percentile
$95\%$ intervals independently resample whole posts within each directional
cohort, retaining their comments together. Full distributions, version
checks, and first-$18$-hour sensitivity analyses appear in
Appendices~\ref{sec:appendix-certainty-comparison}
and~\ref{sec:appendix-certainty-window}.

\subsection{Discussion Progress}
\label{subsec:method-temporal}

For first-day certainty profiles, we order retained verdict comments by
creation time, break ties by comment ID, and divide each post's first
$24$ hours into ten equal-count stages: zero-based rank $r$ among $m$
comments maps to $\lfloor10r/m\rfloor$. We average within-post stage means
equally across posts. Each curve retains a fixed set of posts with valid
certainty for its plotted group in every stage. This prevents changing
post composition but selects discussions with persistent observed
expression; it does not track individuals. The high-agreement AITA overview
and all-agreement community comparison use separate cohorts. Bootstrap
settings and post fixed-effects regressions are specified in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-certainty-progress}.

Participation is distinct from certainty: a stage with verdicts but no
minority has zero minority share and undefined minority certainty.
The supplementary elapsed-time curves instead pool cumulative verdict
counts across posts, giving larger threads greater weight
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-cumulative-participation}).

\subsection{Community Comparison}
\label{subsec:method-community}

We apply identical measures in AITAH. Content and user-overlap comparisons
use the aligned period 17 March 2021--23 December 2025; reception analyses
retain their specified frames. Post embeddings from
\texttt{BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5} \citep{bge_embedding} and
\texttt{intfloat/e5-base-v2} \citep{wang2022text} characterize content overlap;
user intersections, Jaccard overlap, and arrival timing characterize
audiences and activity (Appendices~\ref{sec:appendix-content},
\ref{sec:appendix-overlap}, and~\ref{sec:appendix-timing}).

Cross-posted stories match title-and-body text after Unicode NFKC
normalization, case folding, and whitespace normalization; empty, deleted,
or removed bodies are excluded. Pairs require observed verdict shares in
both communities, without imputation. Contextual case selection additionally
requires at least $20$ retained verdicts per submission. Pair counts appear
in Table~\ref{tab:analytical-samples}. We compare verdict distributions and
parent--reply exchanges without treating shared text as control for audiences,
posting times, or exposure. The community comparison is observational,
not an estimate of the effects of community rules.

Archive gaps and annotation error are not captured by bootstrap intervals;
separate group intervals do not test interactions. Public narratives may
remain identifiable after usernames are removed.
