\section{Data and Methods}
\label{sec:data-method}

\subsection{Data and Measures}
\label{subsec:dataset}
\label{subsec:method}
\label{subsec:method-reception}
\label{subsec:method-op-participation}

We analyze public AITA and AITAH archives containing submissions, comments,
verdict labels, scores, timestamps, and parent relationships. Documented
coverage extends from June 2013 for AITA and March 2021 for AITAH through
December 2025. The main reception cohort includes all eligible posts with
at least $200$ retained top-level verdicts, without post subsampling.
Source processing excludes empty or removed submission bodies;
AutoModerator has no verdict label and does not enter verdict counts.
These records represent retained archive activity, not a community census.
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:analytical-samples} reports sample sizes and exclusions.

A \emph{top-level comment} responds to the original post. Its immediate
children are \emph{direct replies}; all descendants form its reply subtree.
We use top-level verdicts to define the judgment distribution and nested
replies to measure responses to those judgments. Structural measures use
stored labels and linked replies without requiring usable reply text or
verifiable authors; annotation applies additional eligibility criteria.

\paragraph{Verdict alignment.}
We assign the first recognized verdict in textual order, matching short
codes case-sensitively at word boundaries and full phrases case-insensitively.
After normalizing hypothetical forms and H-suffixed aliases, we group
$\mathrm{YA}=\{\mathrm{YTA},\mathrm{ESH}\}$ and
$\mathrm{NA}=\{\mathrm{NTA},\mathrm{NAH}\}$, excluding INFO and
unrecognized comments. Tag meanings and normalization details appear in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-tags}. For post $p$, define
\[
N(p)=N_{\mathrm{YA}}(p)+N_{\mathrm{NA}}(p),\qquad
\rho(p)=N_{\mathrm{YA}}(p)/N(p).
\]
The dominant verdict $D(p)$ is YA when $\rho(p)>.5$ and NA when
$\rho(p)<.5$; exact ties are excluded from alignment-based analyses.
Majority comments match $D(p)$; minority comments oppose it.
High agreement denotes $\rho(p)\le.2$ or $\rho(p)\ge.8$; low agreement
denotes $.4\le\rho(p)\le.6$, excluding ties. Intermediate bands enter
only continuous and all-agreement analyses.

Blaming dissent is minority YA in NA-dominant posts; defending dissent is
minority NA in YA-dominant posts. Directional comparisons thus involve
different story cohorts. Alignment remains fixed to stored final observed
verdict metadata, including in temporal analyses; it does not measure the
opinion distribution perceived by commenters or an official community verdict.

\paragraph{Reception.}
We measure comment score (last recorded upvotes minus downvotes),
direct-reply count (immediate children), and maximum reply depth (levels
below the verdict comment, with zero for no replies). We first average
comments within each post and alignment group, then average these post-level
means with equal weight. Absent sides are undefined, not zero; missing
scores affect only score means. Group means may consequently involve
different contributing post sets and are not necessarily paired contrasts.

To examine reception by arrival timing, we group top-level verdict comments
into hourly bins within $[0,18)$ hours and compute post-equal summaries,
excluding empty post--hour cells. All reception measures use available
archive-wide outcomes rather than a common follow-up duration; grouping by
arrival does not track changes in an individual comment's score or replies.

\subsection{Reply-Pair and Certainty Annotation}
\label{subsec:method-annotation}
\label{subsec:method-rq2}
\label{subsec:method-certainty-contrasts}
\label{subsec:method-temporal}

\paragraph{Direct responses.}
The annotation unit is a top-level verdict comment paired with one immediate
reply. Given the original post as context, DeepSeek labels the reply as
support, challenge, information-seeking, or other relative to its parent,
not the OP or crowd. The prompt includes neither scores nor alignment
metadata. We exclude OP-authored parents or replies, same-author exchanges,
unverifiable identities, and pairs lacking usable post, parent, or reply text.

We draw three seeded samples, stratified by community, high-agreement tail,
and parent alignment, from a broader response frame not assumed to satisfy
the $200$-verdict threshold. Sampling is without replacement within strata,
but samples can overlap and have no post/parent caps. Response proportions
therefore weight pairs and condition on receiving an eligible reply,
unlike post-equal reception means. Challenge denotes disagreement, not
necessarily personal attack. Model annotations underwent development-set
review, not independent held-out validation; prompts, execution settings,
and review details appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-rq2-annotation}.

\paragraph{Expressed certainty.}
We sample $3\%$ of high-agreement and $15\%$ of low-agreement posts from
the $200$-verdict cohort and annotate their top-level comments individually.
The analysis retains the existing export's membership; supplementary draws
exclude previously annotated posts within each community and agreement band.
Certainty ranges from unresolved/conflicting judgments ($1$), through
a leaning allowing alternatives ($2$) and a qualified judgment ($3$), to a
confidently asserted judgment ($4$). The same criteria apply across verdicts
and measure expressed language, not private confidence. Verdict alignment
continues to follow the first-match rule. Annotation documentation and
validation limitations appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-annotation}.

Directional contrasts use all observed minority comments with valid labels,
without an arrival-time cutoff. We average within-post certainty
distributions equally across posts, excluding posts without valid minority
observations. We compare the proportion at level $4$ and mean certainty.
Within each community and agreement band, $95\%$ percentile intervals for
blaming-minus-defending differences independently resample whole posts
within each direction, preserving within-post dependence. The differing
sampling fractions are not used to estimate community-wide certainty.

\paragraph{Discussion progress.}
We order each post's retained first-$24$-hour verdict comments by creation
time, breaking ties by comment ID, and divide them into ten equal-count
stages. Stage means receive equal post weight. Each alignment or direction
uses a fixed set of posts with scored comments in every stage, preventing
changes in post composition but selecting discussions with persistent
observed expression. A stage without scored minority comments has undefined
minority certainty, not zero. These profiles describe successive comments,
not within-person change. The broader all-agreement analysis additionally
includes intermediate-agreement annotated posts without population
reweighting. Detailed stage construction, bootstrap settings, and regression
specifications appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-certainty-progress}.

\subsection{Community Comparison}
\label{subsec:method-community}

We apply the same definitions and measures in AITAH to assess which AITA
patterns recur. Supporting content and audience comparisons use the aligned
period 17 March 2021--23 December 2025; reception analyses retain their
specified frames. Their methods are detailed in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-content}.

Cross-posted stories are identified by identical title-and-body text after
Unicode NFKC normalization, case folding, and whitespace normalization,
excluding empty, deleted, or removed bodies. We require observed verdict
shares in both communities, without imputation; contextual case selection
additionally requires at least $20$ retained verdicts per submission.
Shared text permits comparison around the same narrative but does not
control audiences, posting times, or exposure. Community differences are
therefore observational, not estimates of rule effects.

Bootstrap intervals do not capture archive gaps or annotation error;
separate group intervals do not test interactions. Public narratives may
remain identifiable after usernames are removed.
