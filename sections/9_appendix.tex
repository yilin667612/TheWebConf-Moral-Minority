\label{sec:appendix-tags}

Table~\ref{tab:tags} to their unsuffixed forms. NAH, ESH, and INFO have no
additional H-suffixed variant. Short codes require case-sensitive word
boundaries, preventing matches to lowercase \texttt{nah} or substrings
such as \texttt{ESH} in \texttt{FRESH}; full phrases are case-insensitive.

\section{Community Timing}
\label{sec:appendix-community-timing}

\paragraph{Comment timing.}
\label{sec:appendix-timing}
In the $200$-verdict cohort, median times to $50\%$/$90\%$ of observed
top-level comments are $7.3$/$17.7$ hours in AITA and $6.7$/$20.1$ in AITAH.
Median last-comment times are $52.5$ versus $158.0$ days, but the difference
largely disappears for top-level comments ($32.1$ versus $31.3$ days).
These are archive-relative timings, not complete lifetime follow-up.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/comment_timing.png}
  \caption{Comment timing in AITA and AITAH. Top-level comments mostly
  arrive within the first day; nested replies extend further.}
  \label{fig:timing}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Retrieval timing.}
\label{sec:appendix-retrieval-timing}
Retrieval delay is \texttt{retrieved\_on} minus \texttt{created\_utc}.
Both archives shift around June 2023 from retrospective retrieval, often
after tens or hundreds of days, to near-immediate retrieval from July.
Post-equal weighting does not standardize observation age; absolute reception
values should not be interpreted as common-age or lifetime outcomes.

\section{Certainty Contrasts and Sensitivity}
\label{sec:appendix-certainty-comparison}

Figure~\ref{fig:certainty-low-high} reports post-equal full-period
distributions. Blaming-minus-defending contrasts use $10{,}000$ independent
whole-post resamples within directional cohorts (seed $20260922$), with
replicate-level differences and marginal percentile intervals. These are
not interaction tests. The small high-agreement AITAH defending cohort
is flagged even when its contrast interval excludes zero.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/certainty_distribution_low_high_full_period.pdf}
  \caption{Post-equal certainty distributions (A) and blaming-minus-defending
  certainty-$4$ differences with $95\%$ bootstrap CIs (B). Hollow markers
  flag the small AITAH high-agreement defending cohort.}
  \label{fig:certainty-low-high}
\end{figure}

Restricting high-agreement AITA to \texttt{v4} gives a certainty-$4$
difference of $5.0$ percentage points (CI $[-0.7,10.5]$) and a mean difference
of $0.054$ ($[-0.018,0.123]$). Version and post composition are confounded.
Sensitivity analyses compare full-period estimates with arrivals
in $[0,18)$ hours, using fixed final-verdict labels. The same cutoff in AITAH
defines a comparison window, not a verdict-disclosure event.


Holding the post set fixed to first-18h contributors, adding later comments
reduces the high-agreement mean-certainty gap by $0.019$ points in AITA
(paired-window CI $[-0.035,-0.005]$) and $0.034$ in AITAH
($[-0.061,-0.010]$). This is not a test of convergence at 18 hours or a
causal effect of verdict disclosure. Pooled minority-comment fractions
arriving after 18h are $10.3\%$/$13.4\%$ in AITA/AITAH; after 24h they are
$4.3\%$/$7.0\%$.

\section{Certainty Profiles and Trends}
\label{sec:appendix-certainty-progress}

Profiles use the fixed-stage eligibility rule in
Section~\ref{subsec:method-annotation}. A comment at zero-based rank $r$
among $m$ retained first-day verdicts is assigned to stage
$\lfloor10r/m\rfloor$. Resampling retains each post's complete sequence.
Pointwise intervals use $2{,}000$ post-bootstrap
replicates for all agreement levels (seed $20261007$) and $4{,}000$ for the
high-agreement AITA profile in Figure~\ref{fig:certainty-responses}A
(seed $20261008$). The latter includes separate
majority/blaming/defending cohorts, not a common matched set. Its display
range is $3.2$--$3.9$ on the original $1$--$4$ scale.

Within each all-agreement community--direction cohort, we fit
$\overline C_{p,s}=\alpha_p+\beta(s/9)+\epsilon_{p,s}$ for $s=0,\ldots,9$,
weighting post-stages equally. Thus $\beta$ is the fitted first-to-last-stage
change, not an hourly slope. Intervals use $20{,}000$ whole-post percentile
resamples; two-sided tests use post-cluster standard errors and $G-1$
degrees of freedom. Holm correction covers four slopes; displayed intervals
are unadjusted. Separate slopes do not test between-group differences or
imply monotonic or within-person change. Fixed cohorts favor discussions
with persistent scored expression; available-post profiles have different,
potentially changing compositions.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/certainty_progress_all_agreement_post_fe.pdf}
  \caption{All-agreement certainty profiles (left) and post fixed-effects
  trends (right) in AITA/AITAH. Orange/green: blaming/defending;
  bands/whiskers: $95\%$ post-bootstrap CIs. Stars mark Holm-adjusted
  significance; $n$ counts scored minority comments.}

\section{Cumulative Minority Participation}
\label{sec:appendix-cumulative-participation}

High-agreement $200$-verdict posts contribute retained top-level judgments
with valid nonnegative elapsed times below $24$ hours. At each hourly
checkpoint, cumulative minority counts are divided by cumulative verdict
counts pooled across the cohort, using final alignment. Empty hours add
no counts. These are not post-equal or window-specific shares and have no
uncertainty bands; cumulative aggregation can obscure later variation.


Both AITA directions initially decline; blaming recovers modestly and
defending ends near its initial share. AITAH blaming rises modestly while
defending declines early. These trajectories do not establish withdrawal
by individuals, a regime change, or complete discussion follow-up.

\section{OP Participation}
\label{sec:appendix-op-participation}

Author matching includes top-level and nested comments regardless of verdict,
excluding missing/deleted/removed OP identities and AutoModerator. Within
$0.05$-wide YA-share bins, presence is the post fraction with any OP comment
(pointwise $95\%$ Wilson intervals). First-comment timing is conditional
on presence; each participating post contributes once.

OP presence is $81.0\%$ in AITA and $77.0\%$ in AITAH; among participating
posts, $79.7\%$ and $75.1\%$ have an OP comment within one hour.
Lower presence near unanimous YA is an association, not evidence of
blame-induced withdrawal: OP participation can precede the final verdict.
