\section{Annotation and Analysis Details}
\label{sec:appendix-annotation-details}
\label{sec:appendix-dimensions}

Table~\ref{tab:dimensions} summarizes the analytical dimensions and
measures; Table~\ref{tab:tags} lists the judgment tags used for matching.

\input{components/tab_dimensions}

\runinheading{Annotation samples.}
Certainty annotations sample 3\% of eligible high-agreement posts and 15\%
of low-agreement posts. Reply annotation uses three seeded samples of
16,000 pairs, balanced across community, dominant judgment, and comment
alignment in high-agreement posts; samples may overlap. Pairs involving
the OP, the same author, unverifiable identities, or unusable text are
excluded. Review of 100 development pairs is not held-out validation.
Perspective annotation uses 7,023 eligible pairs from 100 posts, equally
split between NA- and YA-dominant high agreement. Post-specific categories
are derived from 40 sampled comments, then assigned independently to
comments and replies; unmapped texts remain in the denominator.
Full procedures, prompts, and sample counts appear in Supplementary
Sections~\ref{sec:supp-data} and~\ref{sec:supp-annotation}.

\runinheading{Judgment matching.}
\label{sec:appendix-tags}
Short judgment codes are case-sensitive and word-bounded; full phrases
are case-insensitive. Hypothetical and H-suffixed variants are normalized
(Table~\ref{tab:tags}).

\input{components/tab_tags}

\runinheading{Discussion stages.}
\label{sec:appendix-aita-arrival}
First-day judgments are ordered by timestamp (then comment ID for ties)
and divided into ten approximately equal-count stages before splitting
alignment. Groups retain posts represented in every stage, with equal
post weights: 21,978 high-agreement majority, 6,202 high-agreement minority,
and 1,838 low-agreement AITA posts. This selects persistently represented
groups; recorded scores and replies have no common follow-up age.

\runinheading{Inference.}
Reception contrasts use post/group means, CR1 post-cluster standard
errors, and two-sided Wald tests; Holm correction covers nine contrasts
per community. Certainty trends use post fixed effects, progress from
zero to one, and post-cluster tests with post-count minus one degrees
of freedom. Holm correction covers six slopes; the 20,000-replicate
post-bootstrap CIs are unadjusted. These are separate community and
slope estimates, not direct comparisons. Supplementary
Sections~\ref{sec:supp-certainty-analysis} and
\ref{sec:supp-reception-analysis} provide estimation details.

\section{Additional Evidence}

\runinheading{Certainty contrasts and sensitivity.}
\label{sec:appendix-certainty}
Figure~\ref{fig:certainty-low-high} compares full-period post-equal
certainty. The high-agreement AITA certainty-4 gap is 8.0 percentage
points (95\% CI [3.9,12.2]), but restricting annotations to version
\texttt{v4} yields 5.0 points ([-0.7,10.5]); version and post composition
are confounded. The AITAH high-agreement defending sample has 15 posts
for full-period contrasts and eight for stage analysis. No AITAH slope
survives Holm correction (Figure~\ref{fig:certainty-progress}). Detailed
sensitivity checks appear in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:supp-certainty-analysis}.

\runinheading{Judgment distributions.}
\label{sec:appendix-verdict-distribution}
High-agreement posts account for $78.2\%$ of analyzed AITA posts, of which
$80.6\%$ are NA-dominant; AITAH likewise concentrates toward NA dominance.
Post frequency and mean judgment volume per post are distinct.
Figure~\ref{fig:verdict-distributions} shows the distributions.

\runinheading{Reception in AITAH.}
\label{sec:appendix-aitah-reception}
\label{subsec:res-community}
Figure~\ref{fig:aitah-reception-overview} reproduces lower scores and
greater reply engagement for high-agreement minority comments. Blaming
dissent receives lower scores, more replies, and deeper threads than
defending dissent. High-agreement contrasts have Holm-adjusted
$p<.001$; low-agreement reply-count and depth contrasts are not
significant. Shared ordering implies neither equal magnitudes nor a
causal community effect. Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:supp-aitah-reception}
provides additional summaries. Figure~\ref{fig:aitah-arrival-reception}
shows reception across first-day discussion stages.
