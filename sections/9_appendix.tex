\section{Annotation and Analysis Details}
\label{sec:appendix-annotation-details}

\runinheading{Annotation samples.}
Certainty annotations sample 3\% of eligible high-agreement posts and 15\%
of low-agreement posts. Reply annotation uses three seeded samples of
16,000 pairs, balanced across community, dominant judgment, and comment
alignment in high-agreement posts; samples may overlap. Pairs involving
the OP, the same author, unverifiable identities, or unusable text are
excluded. Review of 100 development pairs is not held-out validation.
Perspective annotation uses 7,023 eligible pairs from 100 posts, equally
split between NA- and YA-dominant high agreement. Post-specific categories
are derived from 40 sampled comments, then assigned independently to
comments and replies; unmapped texts remain in the denominator.
Full procedures, prompts, and sample counts appear in Supplementary
Sections~\ref{sec:supp-certainty-annotation}--\ref{sec:supp-samples}.

\runinheading{Judgment matching.}
\label{sec:appendix-tags}
Short judgment codes are case-sensitive and word-bounded; full phrases
are case-insensitive. Hypothetical and H-suffixed variants are normalized
(Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:tags}).

\runinheading{Discussion stages.}
\label{sec:appendix-aita-arrival}
First-day judgments are ordered by timestamp, then comment ID, and divided
into ten approximately equal-count stages before splitting alignment.
Each group retains posts represented in every stage, with equal post
weight. For reception, this retains 21,978 high-agreement majority,
6,202 high-agreement minority, and 1,838 low-agreement AITA posts.
Selection favors persistently represented groups; scores and replies are
archive-wide outcomes, not observations at a common follow-up age.

\runinheading{Statistical comparisons.}
Reception contrasts use post/group means with CR1 post-cluster standard
errors and two-sided Wald tests, with Holm correction across nine
contrasts per community. Certainty trends use post fixed effects and
progress scaled from zero to one; post-cluster tests use post-count minus
one degrees of freedom and Holm correction across six slopes.
Trend CIs use 20,000 whole-post bootstrap replicates and are unadjusted.
These tests do not establish differences between communities or slopes.
Further estimation details appear in Supplementary
Sections~\ref{sec:supp-certainty-analysis} and
\ref{sec:supp-reception-analysis}.

\section{Additional Evidence}
\label{sec:appendix-evidence}

\runinheading{Certainty contrasts and sensitivity.}
\label{sec:appendix-certainty}
Figure~\ref{fig:certainty-low-high} compares full-period post-equal
certainty. The high-agreement AITA certainty-4 gap is 8.0 percentage
points (95\% CI [3.9,12.2]), but restricting annotations to version
\texttt{v4} yields 5.0 points ([-0.7,10.5]); version and post composition
are confounded. The AITAH high-agreement defending sample has 15 posts
for full-period contrasts and eight for stage analysis. No AITAH slope
survives Holm correction. Detailed sensitivity checks and AITAH profiles
appear in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:supp-certainty-analysis}.

\label{sec:appendix-verdict-distribution}
Both communities concentrate toward NA-dominant judgments; post frequency

\runinheading{Reception across agreement.}
\label{sec:appendix-aita-agreement-gradient}
Figure~\ref{fig:aita-agreement-gradient} uses 0.05-wide YA-share bins,
post-equal means, and 2,000 post-bootstrap replicates. Reception gaps
are generally larger toward the endpoints, but reversals near balance
preclude a uniformly monotonic interpretation.

\runinheading{Reception in AITAH.}
\label{sec:appendix-aitah-reception}
\label{subsec:res-community}
Figure~\ref{fig:aitah-reception-overview} reproduces the high-agreement
combination of lower minority scores and greater reply engagement.
Blaming dissent receives lower mean scores, more replies, and deeper
threads than defending dissent. All high-agreement contrasts have
Holm-adjusted $p<.001$; low-agreement reply-count and depth contrasts
are not significant. Shared ordering does not establish equal magnitudes,
greater tolerance, or a causal community effect. Additional summaries
appear in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:supp-aitah-reception}.

\runinheading{Cross-posted examples.}
\label{subsec:res-duplicates}
The bedside-drawer example in the Discussion illustrates considerations
about firearm storage, supervision, and household autonomy, not a
representative community difference. Its posts are not high-agreement
cases, and independently learned taxonomies are not directly comparable.
Matching procedures and exploratory cases appear in Supplementary
Section~\ref{sec:appendix-community-comparison}.

\section{Survey Experiment}
\label{app:survey}

Figure~\ref{fig:survey-procedure} summarizes the between-subjects design.
The four scenarios concern meal payment, a daughter's refusal to prepare
dinner, a late friend, and a workplace email; two have YA majorities and
two NA majorities. Majority/minority labels and agreement percentages
were not displayed. Stimulus properties, questions, and original texts
appear in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:survey-posts},
Table~\ref{tab:survey-questions}, and Section~\ref{app:full-survey-stimuli}.
The 16 unadjusted Welch comparisons are exploratory; post-exposure
alignment groups do not identify causal subgroup effects.
