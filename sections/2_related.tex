\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

We draw on research on minority reception, blame and expression, and moral reasoning in online communities. These strands connect the social treatment of dissent with the reasons exchanged in discussions and the considerations available to readers.

\subsection{Minority Reception in Online Communities}
\label{subsec:related-minority-reception}

Social influence involves both reliance on others as evidence and normative pressures to gain acceptance \cite{deutsch1955normative,toelch2015conformity}. Individuals may align with a unanimous majority even on perceptual tasks \cite{asch1956independence}, yet consistent minorities can also influence majority responses \cite{moscovici1969minority}. Minority influence is more evident on private, indirect measures than on public responses \cite{wood1994minority}, distinguishing influence from public acceptance.

Minority influence can also involve broader consideration rather than adoption of the minority's position. \citet{nemeth1986differential} distinguishes divergent thinking under minority influence from more narrowly focused processing under majority influence. In an anagram experiment, minority exposure promoted the use of multiple strategies and the detection of more correct solutions \cite{nemeth1987divergent}. These findings motivate examining moral considerations introduced in replies and considered by readers; they do not establish that dissent improves the correctness of moral judgments. We distinguish the addition of moral considerations from support or challenge, and readers' consideration of alternatives from agreement with a comment.

Group responses to dissent can combine attention with rejection. \citet{schachter1951deviation} linked persistent dissent to efforts at persuasion and eventual rejection. A replication found rejection and greater overall communication toward dissenters, but no significant interaction between role and time \cite{wesselmann2014revisiting}; a uniform progression from engagement to exclusion cannot be assumed. Meta-analytic evidence also links smaller consistently dissenting factions to stronger rejection \cite{tata1996proportionate}.

Online feedback similarly separates evaluation from engagement. Prior ratings can influence later evaluations \cite{muchnik2013social,glenski2017rating}, and Reddit voting reflects local norms \cite{graham2021sociomateriality}. Responding, disliking, and flagging constitute distinct reactions \cite{kalch2017replying}; downvoted Reddit comments can receive more replies and larger reply trees \cite{davis2021emotional}. Conversation research further distinguishes broad participation from repeated exchanges \cite{backstrom2013threads,choi2015reddit}. Whereas thread-level controversy concerns disagreement or mixed feedback around an item \cite{hessel2019brewing}, our focus is how opposing judgments within a discussion are received. We identify numerical minorities through judgments rather than scores, and distinguish reply volume from support or challenge. These measures characterize reception, not perceived opinion climates, conformity, or persuasion.

\subsection{Blame, Accountability, and Expression}
\label{subsec:related-selective-expression}

Blame is both a judgment of conduct and a socially regulated act. \citet{malle2014blame} describe its dependence on norm violations, causality, intentionality, and reasons or preventability. Public criticism can itself invite scrutiny: communities may demand justification and criticize unwarranted blame \cite{malle2022socially}. Blame also differentiates more finely among mental states than praise, although evidence for greater extremity is weaker \cite{guglielmo2019asymmetric}. Defending against blame is not equivalent to praise, but these accounts motivate examining blaming and defending dissent as distinct positions rather than interchangeable minorities. They do not predetermine which direction receives more acceptance. Research on moral rebels additionally distinguishes reactions from implicated participants and uninvolved observers \cite{monin2008rejection}, motivating attention to responder roles without equating that distinction with OP versus other commenters.

Anticipated evaluation can also shape expression. Accountability may encourage accommodation to a known audience, consideration of alternatives when audience views are unknown, or bolstering of prior commitments \cite{tetlock1989strategies}. Doubtful language is associated with fewer recommendations in online news discussions \cite{evans2023doubt}. These findings motivate studying expressed certainty, but neither blame nor accountability theory implies that one dissent direction must always sound more certain.

Participation may itself be selective. \citet{matthes2010spiral} associate opinion climate with expression among people with low or moderate, but not high, attitude certainty. Anticipated personal attacks are linked to willingness to express minority opinions \cite{neubaum2018sanctions}, while voluntary participation may overrepresent strong views or low conflict avoidance \cite{settle2025promise}. These accounts suggest selection as one possible explanation for aggregate certainty patterns, rather than within-person change. Applying them here requires distinguishing expressed language from private certainty and disagreement from personal attack.

\subsection{Moral Reasoning and Community Context}
\label{subsec:related-aita}

AITA research maps dilemma topics and aggregate judgments \cite{nguyen2022mapping,yudkin2025large}, examines demographic and narrative correlates \cite{decandia2022socialnorms,giorgi2023author}, and links moral disagreement to situational context and social relationships \cite{shi2025moral}. \citet{mellody2025consensus} studies comment valuation as consensus develops. \citet{goglia2024structure} examine growing conversation networks; subsequent work analyzes judgment and language shifts around AITA's 18-hour judgment disclosure, noting participant selection and visibility as obstacles to causal interpretation \cite{goglia2026influence}.

Research on moral reasoning also examines the reasons behind judgments. Social Chemistry 101 represents situation-specific norms as natural-language rules of thumb \cite{forbes2020social}, while Value Kaleidoscope models potentially conflicting values, rights, and duties within a situation \cite{sorensen2024value}. \citet{xi2024morality} extract and categorize rationale elements in AITA comments, relating their distributions to social context. \citet{russo2026pluralistic} distinguish judgment distributions from the moral values expressed in rationales, finding a narrower range of values in LLM responses than in human responses. We investigate how this content develops through interaction by identifying moral considerations that replies add to their parent comments, independently of support or challenge.

Research on subreddit governance and norm interventions motivates attention to community context \cite{fiesler2018rules,matias2019norms}. Our additional AITAH comparison assesses the generalizability of observational reception and expression patterns, including reception around cross-posted stories. Shared narratives do not equalize audiences or exposure, and community differences cannot isolate judgment disclosure from moderation or membership. This comparison is not a natural experiment or a test of whether reader-level experimental effects generalize across communities.
