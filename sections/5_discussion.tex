\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Blaming and Defending Dissent Over Time}
\label{subsec:discussion-temporal-dissent}
The temporal results show that minority voice did not always decline as the discussion progressed. In AITA, blaming dissent generally decreased, while defending dissent increased in the all-agreement and low-agreement discussions. In high-agreement discussions, defending dissent declined at first, but then increased and ended at a level close to where it started.

Agreement level provides further context for these trajectories. Changes in blaming and defending shares were larger in low-agreement AITA discussions, while high-agreement discussions showed smaller absolute changes. The certainty results provide a further observation. Both types of dissent were expressed with greater certainty toward the end of the discussion. Thus, later minority comments were expressed with greater certainty, even when their share of the discussion did not increase.

The community comparison sets a boundary on these findings. Across all agreement levels combined, AITAH showed the opposite directional pattern: blaming share increased while defending share decreased. This suggests that the temporal development of moral dissent may differ across communities. Taken together, these observations suggest that minority participation should be understood in relation to both the direction of dissent and the community in which it occurs.

One possible explanation for the certainty trajectories is selective participation rather than increasing certainty within individuals. Our curves aggregate top-level comments from different authors. Prior research links unfavorable opinion climates to reduced expression particularly among people with lower attitude certainty, and identifies anticipated sanctions as a potential deterrent to minority expression \cite{matthes2010spiral,neubaum2018sanctions}. Voluntary participation can therefore shape whose opinions become visible \cite{settle2025promise}. In AITA's more challenging reception environment, tentative dissenters may be less willing to contribute, leaving later contributions more assertively expressed. The less pronounced certainty increase in AITAH could instead reflect continued entry of more tentative minority voices under a less challenging reception environment. This possibility applies to both blaming and defending dissent.

This account remains a hypothesis. Higher challenge levels alone do not explain an increase across discussion stages; such a trajectory would additionally require changing participation or increasingly visible cues to the costs of dissent. We do not observe who considered commenting but remained silent, what earlier responses commenters saw, or their private certainty. Challenges also need not be personal attacks. Consequently, the community contrast does not establish that anticipated sanctions selected participants, and differences in participants, topics, or expression strategies remain alternative explanations.
